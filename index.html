Why B.E.N. exists

Good communities don’t happen by accident.

They’re built deliberately: with generosity, accountability, curiosity, humour, and people willing to care enough to get involved.

B.E.N. is designed around stewardship rather than status. Leadership exists to protect the network, not to own it. Nobody becomes too important to question, and nobody should need the right connections to belong.

“Authority exists to protect the network, not to own it.”
Our shared compass

The Ten Principles of B.E.N.

Not commandments carved in stone. A common starting point so we can make decisions with the same moral compass.

01
↗

Leave Things Better

Be a net positive.

Try to leave people, places, conversations, and situations better than you found them. We’re here to add something good to the world, not simply take up space in it.

02
◎

People Before Ego

The community matters more than any individual’s status.

Nobody is too important to be challenged, corrected, or held accountable. Leadership is a responsibility, not a badge of importance.

03
⇄

Assume Good Intent — Address Bad Impact

Start with generosity, but don’t ignore harm.

People will make mistakes, misunderstand each other, and occasionally get things wrong. Give people room to explain themselves and learn, while still taking the impact of their actions seriously.

04
✦

Respect Is Non-Negotiable

Disagreement is welcome. Disrespect isn’t.

Challenge ideas, question decisions, and have difficult conversations. But don’t humiliate, harass, intimidate, or deliberately tear people down.

05
◌

Everyone Gets a Seat

Build a community where people can belong without having to earn their way into the clique.

We don’t care who you know, where you come from, how popular you are, or how long you’ve been around. New people should feel welcomed, and established members should help make that possible.

06
→

Bring Solutions, Not Just Problems

Criticism is valuable when it helps us improve.

If something isn’t working, say so. But whenever possible, bring an idea, question, alternative, or next step with you. We want B.E.N. to be a place where people turn frustration into constructive action.

07
＋

Give More Than You Take

Participation is about contribution, not consumption.

Share your time, knowledge, encouragement, skills, connections, creativity, or simply your presence. You don’t have to contribute in the same way as everyone else — just look for ways to add value.

08
⚡

Be Brave Enough to Care

Don’t be afraid to speak up, get involved, or try something new.

Positive communities aren’t built by spectators. Encourage people to take initiative, support each other when things go wrong, and celebrate people who have the courage to make things happen.

09
♥

Keep It Human

People are more important than processes.

Rules and structures exist to help people, not the other way around. Be reasonable, compassionate, and willing to recognise context. We can have standards without losing our humanity.

10
∞

Build Something That Outlives Us

Think beyond today.

B.E.N. shouldn’t depend on a handful of personalities, founders, or friendships. Build relationships, systems, traditions, and a culture that future members can inherit, improve, and eventually pass on themselves.

How we show up

Structure without the power trip.

B.E.N. can be organised without becoming owned by its organisers.

01

Stewardship, not status

Responsibility is a job to do, not a social rank. Leadership remains challengeable, accountable and replaceable.

02

Transparency by default

Important decisions should be understandable. Conflicts of interest are declared. People affected by decisions should have a route to be heard.

03

Designed to outlive founders

Build systems, shared knowledge, handovers and community voice so the network can grow beyond the people who started it.

What kind of network are we building?

Creativity. Benevolence. Meliorism.

Not a collection of cliques. A culture that gives people room to make, contribute, care and improve things together.

B.E.N. is for people who believe creativity is a form of contribution, benevolence is something you practise, and meliorism — the belief that the world can be made better through human effort — is worth acting on. We want ideas to collide, skills to be shared, people to make things together, and optimism to have practical consequences. Whether someone arrives with art, music, games, knowledge, humour, care, time, experience or simply the willingness to help, the aim is the same: turn good energy into something useful, generous and contagious.
